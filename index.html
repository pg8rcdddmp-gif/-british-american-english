📚 THEORY

One language, two varieties

British English and American English are two major varieties of the English language. They share the same basic grammar and most of the vocabulary, but they have developed differently because of history, geography and culture.

🇬🇧 British English

Used mainly in the United Kingdom. It is known for vocabulary such as flat, lift and holiday.

🇺🇸 American English

Used mainly in the United States. Words such as apartment, elevator and vacation are common.

🤝 Still the same language

The differences are real, but speakers usually understand each other without major problems.

🗣️ PRONUNCIATION

They can sound different too

🇬🇧 British pronunciation

Some British accents pronounce the r less strongly when it appears after a vowel.

🇺🇸 American pronunciation

American English often has a more noticeable r sound in words such as car and water.

💡 Important

There isn't one single British or American accent. Both countries have many different regional accents.

📖 GRAMMAR

Grammar differences

Present Perfect

British English often uses the Present Perfect with just, already and yet.


🇬🇧 I've just eaten.
🇺🇸 I just ate.

Collective nouns

British English can use plural verbs with collective nouns, while American English more often uses singular verbs.

Same grammar system

Despite some differences, the basic grammar of both varieties is overwhelmingly shared.

🕰️ HISTORY

How did the differences appear?

1. English in Britain

English developed in Britain over many centuries, influenced by different languages and cultures.

2. English crosses the Atlantic

English settlers brought the language to North America. Over time, the language began changing in its new environment.

3. American English develops

Geographical distance and cultural changes helped create features that became characteristic of American English.

4. The modern world

Today films, music, social media and the internet constantly bring British and American English into contact.
